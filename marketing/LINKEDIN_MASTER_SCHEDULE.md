## 4. Sep 15 (Tue) — C3, Company

```
Responsible travel, for us, mostly comes down to one boring question: who
actually gets to decide about a place, and did we ask.

Our Kamrunag chapter is being rewritten this month with real citations — the
Mandi district administration's own tourism page, three Tribune reports,
Times of India, Hindustan Times, and a peer-reviewed 2018 study of the
Kamrunag Sacred Grove. Not because the chapter was wrong before, but because

calendar and a closure window deserves better than a paraphrase of a
paraphrase.

The argument we keep coming back to: a page that can't say where a cultural
fact came from isn't being respectful of the place, whatever tone it's
written in.

## 6. Sep 19 (Sat) — C4, Company

```
When we can, we cite the source that has to answer for being wrong.

For our Kamrunag chapter, that means the Mandi district administration's
tourism page over a travel blog's paraphrase of it, and a peer-reviewed
floristic study over a blog's description of a peer-reviewed floristic
study. Government pages, court reports and published research aren't always
the most readable sources. They are usually the most accountable ones, and
accountable is what we're optimising for.

It's slower to write this way. We think it's the only version worth
publishing.
```
`#ContentIntegrity #Journalism #Himachal`

## 8. Sep 24 (Thu) — F3, Founder

```
I went through our own story library last week and counted something I
should have counted a long time ago: how many of our 28 stories have a name
attached to them.

Eight are labelled, correctly, as editorial composites — written to
represent something commonly described, not transcribed from one person.
The other twenty are not labelled at all. Read plainly, they read like real
people said these things to us directly.

Zero of the twenty-eight have a named human source.

I don't think anyone set out to mislead a reader. A composite is a
legitimate way to write, and ours are marked as one. The problem is the
twenty that aren't marked either way, sitting on the same site as our temple
etiquette chapter, where we have a rule that a cultural claim without a
named source gets removed, not softened.

We didn't apply our own rule to our own stories. That's the finding,
plainly.

We're fixing it — labelling what's a composite, and working toward finding
named people for the rest.
