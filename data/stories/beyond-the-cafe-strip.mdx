I had booked two nights in Kasol the way you book a city break: cafe recommendations saved, one trek pencilled in, a return ticket already fixed in my head before I had left the taxi.

The cafes were exactly as promised, good coffee, Hebrew menus next to Hindi ones, a crowd that felt like a small temporary country of its own. I liked it more than I expected to. I also finished with it faster than I expected to.

On the second morning a homestay owner mentioned, almost as an aside, that his cousin ran rooms in a village an hour's walk up the ridge, no road, no wifi worth mentioning. I went because I had nothing better scheduled, which is probably the only honest reason anyone discovers anything.

The village was not dramatic. That was the point. Apple trees, a woman calling her son in for the evening, a temple I did not fully understand and was not rushed to. Nobody was performing authenticity at me. They were just living somewhere, and I happened to be standing in it.

I came back down two days later than planned, having seen a fraction of the valley and understood, finally, that Kasol was never the destination. It was the door. What was on the other side of it did not care whether I had booked a return ticket.
