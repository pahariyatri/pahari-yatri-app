Our guide told the Alexander's-army story on the way up to Malana with the total confidence of something he had said a thousand times, Greek soldiers, a lost battalion, blue eyes as proof. I had read enough beforehand to know no serious source actually confirms it, but I let him finish. It was a good story, told well, and I understood why it survives.

On the way down, I asked him a different question: what did he think tourists misunderstood most about the village. He was quiet for a while before answering, which was itself an answer.

He said most visitors come wanting either the myth or the marijuana, and almost nobody asks about the language, which he considers the village's actual treasure, spoken nowhere else on earth and slowly thinning as younger residents leave for education and work elsewhere. He talked about the council structure, the way decisions still move through it, and how little of that ever makes it into anyone's travel post.

He did not want to discuss the cannabis economy in detail, and I did not push him to. It was clear from how quickly the subject closed that it carries weight in the village that has nothing to do with a traveller's curiosity about it.

I left Malana having learned less about Alexander the Great and more about a language I will probably never hear again outside that valley. That felt like the correct trade.
