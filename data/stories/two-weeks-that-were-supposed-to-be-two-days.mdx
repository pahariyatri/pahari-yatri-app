I am a graphic designer from Berlin, and I had exactly two nights budgeted for Kalga on a longer route through Himachal. I am writing this from Kalga, considerably later than planned.

What kept me was not any single dramatic thing. It was the accumulation of small ones. The homestay owner's daughter teaching me three words of the local dialect and laughing, correctly, at my pronunciation. The rhythm of the village settling into evening, wood smoke, someone calling cows in, a specific quality of cold that arrives the moment the sun drops behind the ridge.

I had travelled through enough mountain towns to know the difference between a place performing charm for visitors and a place that simply continues, indifferent to whether you are watching. Kalga is the second kind. Nobody here is managing an experience for me. They are managing apple trees, homestays, and a winter that arrives on its own schedule regardless of my plans.

I rebooked my onward train four times. Each time felt like a small, private negotiation with myself about what I actually wanted from this trip versus what I had planned before I understood anything about the place.

I do not know if I would recommend two weeks in Kalga to everyone. I know that arriving with a fixed schedule and leaving with a much looser one seems to be a fairly common experience here, based on the other travellers I met who had done exactly the same thing, for their own reasons.
