# Manali Travel Guide: The Local Reality

Manali is two different towns. One is the Mall Road—a chaotic mix of neon signs and heavy traffic. The other is Old Manali and the surrounding villages like Vashisht, which still hold onto stone-and-wood architecture and ancient hot springs. 

For locals, Manali isn't a "hill station" for selfies; it’s a strategic base. It’s where you gather your permits, check your bike’s carburetors, and wait for the Rohtang or Shinku La passes to open. 

If you come here expecting total isolation, you’ll be disappointed. But if you use it as a launching pad for the deeper Lahaul and Spiti valleys, it is an indispensable part of the Himalayan journey.

## Who Manali Is Actually For
- **Adventurers**: Heading to Ladakh or Spiti.
- **Digital Nomads**: Who need fast fiber-optics amidst the cedars.
- **Social Seekers**: Travelers who enjoy the "cafe culture" and international social scenes.

## Who Should Avoid Manali
- **Peace-seekers**: During the May-June honeymoon rush.
- **Slow travelers**: Who hate traffic jams on mountain roads.

## Nearby Places Locals Prefer
- **Naggar**: 20km away; home to a medieval castle and the quietest art galleries in the state.
- **Sajla**: A village with a sacred waterfall and zero commercial noise.

## Common Tourist Mistakes
- Staying strictly on the Mall Road.
- Booking a hotel without parking in peak season.
- Ignoring the local weather warnings for the high passes.

## Local Reality FAQ
- **When does Rohtang Pass open?** Usually late May, depending on the snow clearing by BRO.
- **Is Old Manali better than New Manali?** For yatris, yes. It's more walkable and has a better vibe.
- **Are there ATMs?** Plenty in the main town, fewer and less reliable in Old Manali.
- **Can I rent a bike?** Yes, Manali is the hub for RE Himalayan rentals. Check the tires and clutch before you pay.
- **Is the Atal Tunnel always open?** Usually, though it may close during heavy blizzards.
