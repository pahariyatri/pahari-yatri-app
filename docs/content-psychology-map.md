# Content Psychology Map — how the ad-strategy docs apply to Pahari Yatri

Source material: the HOOK / PSYCH / PERSONA / SCRIPT / STRAT / DISTRIBUTION decks + TPC Ad Breakdown (July 2026). This document translates those ad frameworks into **library content practice** and records how the story archive now implements them.

## 1. What we took from each framework

**4-Layer Hook Stack** (Stop → Relate → Believe → Continue): every story now opens with a *recognition* line before any scenery. Not "the mountains were beautiful" but "Our startup died on a Tuesday" — the reader's own life is the pattern interrupt.

**7 Hook Types**, adapted from ad copy to literary openings:
- *Permission Slip* → "You are not a bad mother for wanting three days of silence." (River Hymn)
- *Villain Swap* → "The reason I couldn't enjoy mountains was never my fitness. It was my watch." (Churdhar Bell Echo)
- *Bold Claim* → "There is gold in Kamrunag lake and nobody counts it." (Mirror of Stillness)
- *Curiosity Gap* → "A twelve-year-old ruined my week in the best way." (Parashar Floating Island)
- *Before & After* → "Before Manali, winter: my life was a group project everyone had opinions on." (Solstice Fire)

**WHEN personas** (the key upgrade): stories are no longer narrated by an anonymous poetic "we." Each has a narrator defined by *the moment that put them on the trail* — the thing that happened last Wednesday: a promotion lost, a visa approved, a bypass surgery, a clean scan, a dead startup, a voice that gave out mid-lecture. Demographics describe a person; the WHEN describes why they searched today. Readers arrive at these stories in their own WHEN moments — that's the match that converts a scroller into a reader into a Yatri.

Distribution: all three self-anchors covered; all four valence zones appear (High Negative used sparingly and always resolved — this brand never ends in fear). Language intensity: uniformly Low (conversational, observational) — correct for the brand; do not add urgency language.

**Episodic content / Zeigarnik loops**: books→chapters→stories is a native series format. Now mechanically wired:
- chapter → *next chapter in the book* card ("The book continues")
- chapter → parent book card ("This chapter belongs to")
- chapter ↔ stories both directions; story → next story
No page is a dead end. The open loop is the return mechanism — same reason "Will It Blend" worked as a show, not a video.

## 2. Rules going forward (for new stories via /contribute or /admin)

1. **Open with the WHEN**, not the mountain. First two lines must let a reader say "that's me."
2. **One narrator, one voice field.** Archetypal voices ("a bank manager from Solan, 40"), never invented full names or fake credentials. When real contributed stories arrive, real voices (with permission) replace archetypes — mark them so.
3. **Pick an under-covered cell** before writing (see table). Never two consecutive publishes in the same cell.
4. **The turn is mandatory**: every story needs its Layer-3 belief shift (the villain swap: it wasn't the phone / the fitness / the crowd — it was the watch, the plan, the question).
5. **Close the loop**: end on the payoff + one practical grace note (best season, one etiquette rule) woven into voice, never a checklist.
6. **Keep intensity low.** No urgency, no "don't miss," no exclamation marks. The brand's persuasion is recognition, not pressure.

## 3. Reels bridge (from the DISTRIBUTION/HOOK decks)

Each story's opening line is a ready-made reel text-hook, and its WHEN moment defines the target viewer. Under Andromeda, visually distinct first-3-seconds matter more than copy variants — so shoot each story's reel in its *narrator's world* (a bank desk, a school corridor, a hospital waiting room, a wedding shoot) cutting to the trail. That gives every reel a distinct visual signature while the format stays serial: "Why I walked" as an episodic franchise, one WHEN per episode, always ending "read the full story in the Library — link in bio."
