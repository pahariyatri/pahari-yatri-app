# Parvati Valley cluster: first-pass research record (Kalga, Pulga, Tosh, Malana, Kheerganga)

Research date: 2026-09-08 (IST). Scope: public web research only — no first-hand visit, no local interview, no paid access. This is a **first-pass** dossier (2-3 sources per topic), lighter than the Kamrunag standard (`research-record.md`, 6 independent source groups). It is sufficient to plan and to avoid inventing facts, but **not** sufficient to clear a 1,500-2,000 word chapter for publication without a deeper pass — treat it the way `research-record.md` treats Kamrunag: a gate, not a green light.

Evidence classification follows the same five labels used for Kamrunag: **VERIFIED FACT**, **LOCAL TRADITION**, **ORAL HISTORY** (none collected here), **EDITORIAL INTERPRETATION**, **UNVERIFIED CLAIM**.

## Malana — handle with extra care

Malana is the most mythologized village in this cluster, and several claims that circulate as fact are explicitly **not settled** even in the sources reporting them.

| Field | Evidence | Confidence / gate |
|---|---|---|
| Location/altitude | **VERIFIED FACT (source-reported):** ~2,600 m, Kullu district, Parvati valley side.[8] | Medium. |
| Language | **VERIFIED FACT (source-reported):** roughly 2,000 residents speak Kanashi, a Sino-Tibetan language spoken nowhere else.[8] | Medium-high — reported by an encyclopedic source; worth an academic-linguistics cross-check before publishing as certain. |
| Self-governance / "parliament" | **LOCAL TRADITION, reported as living practice:** villagers describe a two-house local council (Kanishthang / Jayeshthang) with decisions taken by invoking Jamlu Devta through a village oracle.[9][8] | Medium as reported local practice. **"Possibly the oldest democracy in the world" is an UNVERIFIED CLAIM** — it is a popular framing repeated by travel sites, not a historical or political-science finding. If used at all, it must be attributed as "some visitors and writers call it..." — never asserted as fact. |
| Jamlu Devta / Jamadagni Rishi | **LOCAL TRADITION:** villagers worship Jamlu Devta, identified with the sage Jamadagni.[9][8] | Medium — this directly connects to real GSC search demand: `jamadagni rishi temple` already gets impressions on pahariyatri.com (see below). A Malana/Parvati chapter that handles this connection carefully is a genuine internal-linking opportunity to the temple cluster — but the Jamlu/Jamadagni identification itself stays LOCAL TRADITION, not verified genealogy. |
| Alexander's-army origin myth | **UNVERIFIED CLAIM — explicitly disputed even by sources that repeat it.** Multiple sources report the legend that Malana's residents descend from soldiers Alexander the Great left behind in 326 BCE, and each one states this is unproven.[10][11][8] | **Do not present as history under any framing.** If mentioned at all, it must be labeled a legend/popular claim with the "nothing has been proven definitively" caveat kept intact — this is exactly the kind of claim `local-verification-editor` exists to catch. |
| "Malana Cream" | Real product/reputation exists in outside reporting, but it names a controlled substance. | **Do not write about this at all in a Pahari Yatri chapter.** It has no responsible-travel framing and sits outside the brand's territory; omission is the correct editorial choice, not a gap to fill. |
| No-touch customs toward outsiders | **LOCAL TRADITION, widely reported:** visitors are asked not to touch village buildings, walls or residents.[12][8] | Medium-high as reported etiquette; treat as the single most important practical/responsible-travel fact for this village — it is exactly the kind of "what a Yatri should understand before going" content the brand wants, and it needs a specific, current statement rather than a vague "be respectful." |

## Kheerganga — legend vs. current management (existing chapter, needs a hold)

The live chapter (`kheerganga-buni-buni-pass.yaml`) already tells the Kartikeya story and separately states hot-spring rules. Fresh research confirms two things worth flagging before any rewrite:

1. **The legend itself has multiple inconsistent versions even within tourism sources** — some center on Kartikeya sulking after losing a race to Ganesha, others on a round-the-world contest, with Shiva, Parvati or Parshuram cast differently across retellings.[13][14] This is **LOCAL TRADITION with variants**, not a single canonical story — a rewritten chapter should say "one version of the story..." rather than presenting one variant as definitive, exactly as `research-record.md` recommends for Kamrunag's Barbarika equivalence.
2. **Some sources claim the spring "relieves all ailments."[14]** This is an **UNVERIFIED CLAIM** and must not enter any Pahari Yatri copy — it reads as a health claim, which the brand cannot make about an untested natural hot spring. The current chapter correctly avoids this; a rewrite must keep avoiding it.
